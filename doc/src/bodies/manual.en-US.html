🎯 Introduction

NuMonitor is a serial port monitor for Arduino, ESP32, modems and general embedded work. It replaces the Arduino IDE Serial Monitor with live charting, GPS tracking and full RS-232 signal control — all from a single HTML file that runs directly in the browser.

Nothing to install. Run it online at nuxtack-tw.github.io/NuMonitor4SerialPort, or download the single HTML file and open it locally.

💻 Requirements

Browser support

NuMonitor is built on the Web Serial API, which is only implemented in Chromium-based browsers. The API also requires a secure context: HTTPS, localhost, or a local file:// page — all three work.

Hardware

Anything that presents itself as a serial port: Arduino boards, ESP32 modules, USB-to-serial adapters (CH340/CH343, CP210x, FTDI, PL2303), cellular modems, industrial equipment with an RS-232 port, and so on. NuMonitor recognises around twenty common USB-serial chipsets by VID:PID and shows a friendly name.

🚀 Quick start

Step 1 — Open NuMonitor

Either open the hosted version, or open NuMonitor4SerialPort.html from a local copy. No installation, no build step.

Step 2 — Connect a device

  1. Plug the device into a USB port
  2. Click Connect
    • First time: the browser shows its port picker — choose your device
    • After that: it reconnects to the same port automatically, no dialog
  3. To use a different port, click the ▾ next to the button and pick one from the list, or choose Other… (browser picker) to authorise a new device
  4. Once connected the button turns green “Connected” with a slow breathing glow and the port menu arrow disappears. Hover the button and it turns red — click to Disconnect
  5. If the device is unplugged the button turns orange “Connecting…” and retries every second until it comes back; clicking it then cancels the retry

Step 3 — Set the baud rate

Pick the rate that matches your device — for Arduino sketches that is whatever you passed to Serial.begin(). Available rates: 300, 1200, 2400, 4800, 9600, 19200, 38400, 57600, 74880, 115200 (default), 230400, 250000, 500000, 1000000 and 2000000 baud.

The baud rate is saved together with the port, so a page reload restores both.

Garbled characters? That is almost always a baud rate mismatch. Ports are opened as 8N1 (8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit), which is what the overwhelming majority of devices use.

URL detection

Links appearing in the stream become clickable automatically: https://, http://, www. (https is assumed) and ftp://.

Line endings

The selector to the right of the input box controls what is appended to everything you send:

OptionBytes sentUse it for
None(nothing appended)When you control every byte yourself
LF\n (0x0A)Arduino/ESP32 sketches using readStringUntil('\n') — the default
CR\r (0x0D)Modem AT commands and most CLI firmware
CRLF\r\nWindows-style devices and some modules
⚠️ Talking to a modem? Select CR. The AT command set terminates commands with a carriage return. Send LF or nothing and the modem keeps waiting for the terminator — you get no response at all. That is not a connection failure; the command was simply never considered complete.

This setting is remembered and restored the next time you open the page.

🚦 RS-232 signal panel

A row of handshake indicators appears under the header once you are connected; while disconnected the whole row is hidden.

LOCAL (OUT) — your outputs, click to toggle

IndicatorFull nameNotes
BRKBreakAsserts a break condition. Click once to start, again to release
RTSRequest To SendUsually required by devices doing hardware flow control
DTRData Terminal ReadyMost modems will not accept commands unless DTR is asserted

Why do they start unlit? The Web Serial API can set output signals but cannot read them back. Until you click one, NuMonitor genuinely does not know its state, so it shows the honest answer rather than guessing what the browser did on open.

⚠️ Arduino and ESP32 users: on most development boards DTR and RTS drive the auto-reset circuit, so toggling them may reboot the board. NuMonitor deliberately never touches these signals on connect — the decision is entirely yours.

REMOTE (IN) — device outputs, read-only

IndicatorFull nameLit means
DCDData Carrier DetectCarrier present — a modem has established a link
DSRData Set ReadyThe device is powered and ready
CTSClear To SendThe device is willing to accept data
RIRing IndicatorA ring signal was detected

These four are polled every 250 ms — the Web Serial API exposes no signal-change events, so polling is the only option.

ACT and ERR

  • ACT — blinks whenever bytes move in either direction. During continuous transfer it appears to stay lit
  • ERR — turns red and blinks for three seconds on a framing, parity, buffer-overrun or received-break condition; click it to clear early. The connection is not dropped — reading resumes automatically

About parity errors: ports are currently opened as 8N1 — without a parity bit a parity error cannot occur, so in practice ERR responds to framing, overrun and break conditions.

📈 Plotter

Automatic parsing

Nothing needs to be configured. Print plain text from the device and NuMonitor decides what to chart: any line containing at least one parsable number becomes data; anything else stays plain terminal text.

Chart controls

ButtonWhat it does
PauseFreezes the chart while data keeps arriving in the background
SmoothDraws line and area charts as smooth curves
ClearDiscards the data for that group
CSVExports the group's data as a CSV file
BackgroundSets a custom chart background colour, remembered per group
MapAppears only when the group contains map: coordinates

X-axis cursor

Move the pointer across the chart to read the exact value of every series at that sample.

Scroll to zoom

Scrolling over a chart changes how many samples are displayed, from 20 up to 2000 (100 by default). Up to 500 samples per series are retained in memory.

Legend interaction

Click a legend entry to hide or show that series; click its colour swatch to change the colour, which is remembered.

📊 Chart types

Note: map is not a valid chart type. Writing {X|0|1|map} falls back to line. The map is a view toggle that appears on any tab whose data carries coordinates.

🗺️ GPS tracking

Enabling the map

Append map:latitude,longitude to any data line. A 🗺️ button appears on that tab.

Map controls

  • Trail points — how many recent positions stay on the map, from 1 to 100 (10 by default)
  • Follow — keeps the newest position centred; turn it off to pan and zoom freely without the map snapping back
  • Clicking a trail marker shows the coordinates and the data values recorded at that point

📝 Data format reference

Full syntax

{GroupName|Ymin|Ymax|chartType}#counter Label:Value Label=Value map:lat,lng

Fields

FieldRequiredNotes
Group nameNoBecomes a tab. Any text, including non-Latin scripts
Y min / Y maxNoA number, or auto. Anything unparsable is treated as auto
Chart typeNoline area stack scatter bar gauge pie; anything else falls back to line
#counterNoImmediately after }. Labels the X axis; negatives and decimals accepted
Data pointsYesAt least one parsable number, otherwise the line stays plain text
map:NoAdds the map view to that tab

Writing data points

Serial.println("Temp:25.5 Humidity:60.2"); // Key:Value — recommended Serial.println("Temp=25.5 Humidity=60.2"); // Key=Value Serial.println("25.5 60.2 1013.2"); // bare numbers → CH1, CH2, CH3 Serial.println("Temp\t25.5"); // Label [tab/space] Value
  • Separators: space, comma or tab
  • Label/value separator: : or =
  • Units are stripped automatically — 25.5°C, 60.2% and 40.5km/h all parse to their leading number
  • Unparsable tokens are skipped without discarding the rest of the line

Example sketches

// Environment monitoring with units and a sample counter void loop() { Serial.print("{Environment|0|100|line}#"); Serial.print(seq++); Serial.print(" Temp:"); Serial.print(readTemp(), 1); Serial.print("°C"); Serial.print(" RH:"); Serial.print(readRH(), 1); Serial.println("%"); delay(100); // 10 Hz } // Three tabs at once void loop() { Serial.print("{Temperature|15|35|line}Indoor:"); Serial.print(t1); Serial.print(" Outdoor:"); Serial.println(t2); Serial.print("{System|0|100|bar}CPU:"); Serial.print(cpu); Serial.print(" MEM:"); Serial.println(mem); Serial.print("{Battery|0|100|gauge}Level:"); Serial.println(bat); delay(500); } // Drone telemetry with a GPS trail void loop() { Serial.print("{Drone|0|120|line}#"); Serial.print(frame++); Serial.print(" Alt:"); Serial.print(alt, 1); Serial.print(" Speed:"); Serial.print(spd, 1); Serial.print(" map:"); Serial.print(lat, 6); Serial.print(","); Serial.println(lng, 6); delay(200); }

❓ FAQ

Q: No ports are listed.

A: Check that the device is plugged in, the USB driver is installed, you are using Chrome/Edge/Opera, and that no other program is holding the port — the Arduino IDE Serial Monitor is the usual culprit.

Q: I only get garbled characters.

A: The baud rate does not match. Set NuMonitor to the same rate as your device's Serial.begin().

Q: No chart appears.

A: Check that the plotter toggle (📊) is on, that the line contains at least one parsable number, and that you have not already hit the 10-group limit.

Q: My modem does not answer AT commands at all.

A: Work through these in order:

  1. Set the line ending to CR — by far the most common cause. AT commands must end with a carriage return; with the default LF the modem never responds
  2. Assert DTR (and RTS if the modem uses hardware flow control) from the signal panel. Other terminal programs raise these automatically on open; NuMonitor deliberately does not, to avoid resetting development boards
  3. Check DSR and CTS — DSR dark means the device is unpowered or miswired; CTS dark means it is not accepting data
  4. Confirm the baud rate
  5. Turn on echo and watch the TX bytes counter to prove the data actually left the computer

Q: What is the difference between auto-reconnect and auto-connect?

A: One switch governs two behaviours:

  • Device unplugged while connected → retries every second, indefinitely, until it returns
  • Page reloaded or reopened → reconnects to the last port and restores its baud rate

If you disconnected deliberately before leaving, it will not reconnect on its own. The switch state itself is remembered too.

Q: ESP32 connections are unstable.

A: ESP32 USB CDC devices can invalidate their port object after a reboot. NuMonitor discards the stale handle and re-acquires the port through the browser, which is why reconnection works without you re-picking the device.

Q: How do I export data?

A: Click CSV in the chart's control row to export that group.

Q: How do I switch theme or language?

A: Use the moon/sun toggle and the language selector at the top right. Both are remembered. Chart and terminal colours follow the theme.

The Manual link and the version badge follow the interface language too. This manual is available in English, 日本語, Tiếng Việt, Bahasa Indonesia, Bahasa Melayu and 繁體中文 — pick one from the selector at the top right. Interface languages without their own manual yet fall back to English.
